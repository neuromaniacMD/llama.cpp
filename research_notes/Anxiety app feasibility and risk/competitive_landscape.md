## 1. Market size, growth, and app-store saturation

### Takeaway
Analyst firms put the global mental health app market at roughly USD 7.5–8.5B for 2024–2025, with CAGRs of about 14–15% to 2030. "Depression and anxiety management" is the largest segment at about 28–30% (≈USD 2.2–2.4B). These are paywalled syndicated reports with opaque methods, so treat them as directional only. App stores hold roughly 10,000–20,000 mental health apps, a figure that has not been rigorously updated since about 2018.

### Cited Findings
- Grand View Research: global mental health apps market is forecast to reach about USD 17.52B by 2030, at a 14.6% CAGR for 2025–2030 — [GII / Grand View listing](https://giiresearch.com/report/grvi1679420-mental-health-apps-market-size-share-trends.html); [MarketResearch.com / Grand View](https://marketresearch.com/Grand-View-Research-v4060/Mental-Health-Apps-Size-Share-40256947)
- Secondary pages cite Grand View's 2024 base as about USD 7.48B. One secondary summary puts the depression & anxiety management segment at USD 2,151.5M (28.7% share) in 2024. Grand View says this segment "dominated" in 2023 — [GII / Grand View](https://www.gii.co.jp/report/grvi1511975-mental-health-apps-market-size-share-trends.html) (base-year figure only from secondary pages; not verified in primary report)
- Global Market Insights: USD 8.2B in 2025, with depression & anxiety management the top-revenue segment at USD 2.4B in 2025 — [GMInsights](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/mental-health-apps-market)
- Persistence Market Research: USD 8,006.9M in 2025 — [Persistence](https://persistencemarketresearch.com/article/mental-health-apps-2025.asp)
- Roots Analysis: USD 8.54B in 2025 — [Roots Analysis](https://www.rootsanalysis.com/reports/mental-health-apps-market.html)
- Other publishers with their own estimates (not extracted): [Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/mental-health-apps-market-analysis), [Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/mental-health-apps-market). GMI also has a separate "chatbot-based mental health apps" market report — [GMInsights chatbot](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/chatbot-based-mental-health-apps-market)
- App count: "around 20,000 mental health apps" in app stores (2026 paper) — [JMIR Human Factors 2026](https://humanfactors.jmir.org/2026/1/e67944). A PMC review gives "between 10,000 and 20,000" — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091910). APA (2019): >10,000 — [APA blog](https://www.psychiatry.org/news-room/apa-blogs/mental-health-apps-evidence-not-so-plentiful). NAMI (2021): ~10,000 — [NAMI](https://www.nami.org/Blogs/NAMI-Blog/June-2021/How-To-Navigate-the-Overwhelming-Volume-of-Mental-Health-Apps)
- The ">20,000" figure is traced to a 2018 study by Schueller and colleagues and has been re-cited ever since. One systematic store search found only 1,435 mental health apps, so counts depend heavily on definitions — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7801857); [Frontiers Digital Health 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.1003181)
- Long tail: an older IMS analysis found about half of Android health apps had fewer than 500 downloads — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/ims-half-android-health-apps-have-fewer-500-downloads)
- Usage concentration: a user-adjusted analysis (National Elf Service summary) found that a small number of apps account for most active use — [National Elf Service](https://nationalelfservice.net/treatment/digital-health/mental-health-apps-user-adjusted-analyses)

### Inferences
- 2025 estimates from four publishers fall in a tight band (≈USD 8.0–8.5B). The likely reason is shared secondary inputs, not independent validation. Their scope is also inconsistent: some include meditation/wellness subscriptions such as Calm, which make up most consumer revenue, and some exclude therapy platforms. The ≈USD 1.1B mental-health revenue that Teladoc alone reported for 2025 (see §2) is about 13% of the whole "app market", which suggests the definitions do not cover therapy marketplaces consistently.
- The anxiety-specific slice (≈USD 2.2–2.4B) is the closest analyst proxy for the addressable market. Most of it is general wellness/meditation spend, not clinically validated anxiety treatment.
- The app count implies extreme long-tail crowding. Competition for downloads is saturated, while competition among clinically validated, reimbursed anxiety products is thin.

### Gaps
- No Sensor Tower / Appfigures / data.ai download or revenue figures for 2025–2026 were retrieved. Category-level download and consumer-spend data is still missing.
- No anxiety-only market sizing (as distinct from "depression & anxiety") from a reputable source.
- Grand View's own 2025 base-year figure was not confirmed from the primary page.

## 2. Leading players: users, revenue, funding, positioning

### Takeaway
Two scale tiers dominate: (a) wellness/meditation subscriptions (Calm, Headspace) and (b) therapy marketplaces (BetterHelp/Teladoc, Talkspace). In 2026 both tiers are consolidating at sharply reduced valuations. Headspace was sold to Sword Health for up to ~USD 300M (it was valued at ~USD 3B in 2021). Talkspace agreed to be bought by UHS for ~USD 835M EV. Evidence-based AI chatbots have either retreated to B2B (Wysa: >80% of revenue from businesses and health plans) or exited consumer entirely (Woebot, June 30, 2025). General-purpose LLMs, chiefly ChatGPT at ~800M weekly users, now act at massive scale as a de facto, unregulated mental-health channel.

**General-purpose LLMs (ChatGPT) as mental-health channel**
- OpenAI disclosure (Oct 27, 2025): ~800M weekly active users. ~0.15% show explicit indicators of suicidal planning/intent (>1M people/week), ~0.15% show heightened emotional attachment, and ~0.07% show possible psychosis/mania signs (~560K/week). OpenAI worked with >170 clinicians to change model responses — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/openai-says-over-a-million-people-talk-to-chatgpt-about-suicide-weekly); [Platformer](https://www.platformer.news/openai-mental-health-research-chatgpt-suicide-delusions/)
- Rock Health's 2025 maturity-curve report flags "AI chatbots for mental health" as an emerging area, and notes Slingshot AI's USD 93M raise as a signal of investor conviction in purpose-built AI therapy — [HIT Consultant on Rock Health](https://hitconsultant.net/2025/12/08/rock-health-2025-report-mental-health-ai-and-longevity-tech-lead-innovation-curve/)

### Inferences
- Consumer wellness and DTC therapy have both peaked. Exit valuations are a fraction of 2021 marks: Headspace at ~10% of its 2021 valuation, and Talkspace at ~60% of its SPAC valuation (EV basis). Buyers are diversified care platforms (Sword) and provider systems (UHS) that want distribution and clinician networks, not standalone apps.
- The surviving consumer moats are brand + content + habit (Calm) and therapist-supply marketplaces (BetterHelp). A new anxiety app is unlikely to win on content/meditation breadth.
- ChatGPT's free, always-on scale sets the consumer bar for "conversational support". An evidence-based app has to show what an LLM lacks: structured protocols, outcome measurement, clinician escalation and regulatory standing.
- Woebot's exit shows that the FDA path is too costly for a consumer AI chatbot without a reimbursement payoff.

## 3. Prescription digital therapeutics (PDTs) for anxiety and adjacent conditions

### Takeaway
The US PDT model has a small number of FDA-cleared products, among them Big Health's DaylightRx (GAD, Sept 2024) and Otsuka/Click's Rejoyn (MDD, 2024), but reimbursement is weak. Pear Therapeutics' 2023 bankruptcy, after payers refused to cover it, is the defining cautionary tale. FDA clearance is necessary for a "prescription" claim but does not get a product paid for.

### Cited Findings
- **DaylightRx (Big Health):** FDA clearance announced Sept 4, 2024. It is an adjunct to usual care for GAD in adults 22+, called the first FDA-cleared digital treatment for GAD. The 90-day digital CBT program covers applied relaxation, stimulus control (worry time), cognitive restructuring and exposure. Big Health reports >70% remission in RCTs, with effects lasting ≥6 months (company claim). Clearance draws on RCTs including one against psychoeducation. It is Big Health's second clearance after SleepioRx (insomnia, Aug 2024), and it is available on a licensed provider's order — [Big Health press release](https://www.bighealth.com/news/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-daylightrx); [Drug Topics](https://drugtopics.com/view/fda-clears-first-digital-therapeutic-for-treatment-of-generalized-anxiety-disorder); [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-daylightrx); backer [Gilde Healthcare](https://gildehealthcare.com/news/all/gilde-healthcare-company-big-health-receives-us-fda-clearance-for-daylightrx/)
- **Rejoyn (Otsuka / Click Therapeutics):** the first FDA-cleared app for depression (2024). It is an adjunct to clinician-managed outpatient care for adults 22+ on antidepressants, based on a 354-adult trial in partial responders. Psychiatric News flagged cost and efficacy as "unclear"; a critical outlet reports a ~USD 220 price — [Psychiatric News](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.pn.2024.06.6.15); [Mad in America](https://madinamerica.substack.com/p/fda-clears-new-phone-app-for-negative)
- **Pear Therapeutics:** filed Chapter 11 in 2023 after payers refused to cover its products. Assets were auctioned: PursueCare acquired reSET/reSET-O (and later raised a USD 20M Series B), and Harvest Bio bought trademarks and PearConnect/reSET assets — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/pursuecare-revamps-pear-therapeutics-reset-reset-o-digital-therapeutics); [Psychiatric News](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.pn.2024.06.6.15); Pear FY2022 10-K — [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835567/000183556723000018/pear-20221231.htm)

### Inferences
- Pear failed because coverage, not clearance, was the bottleneck. Woebot then retreated from FDA ambitions in 2025. Together they suggest the US prescription route for anxiety is open (DaylightRx proves the regulatory pathway) but commercially unproven. Any new entrant will need a payer or employer channel rather than physician prescribing alone.
- DaylightRx sets the evidence and feature bar for GAD: a multi-RCT CBT protocol plus clearance. A new entrant targeting GAD would face a cleared incumbent. Panic disorder, social anxiety and health anxiety had no comparable cleared product in the results found.

### Gaps
- **Freespira** (breathing/capnometry device for panic disorder and PTSD): no search results retrieved. Background knowledge, unverified here: FDA-cleared for panic disorder (≈2013/2018) and later PTSD, with some payer contracts.
- Status of the CMS digital mental health treatment (DMHT) billing codes (2025 Physician Fee Schedule), and whether DaylightRx has gained meaningful coverage: not researched.
- DaylightRx uptake and revenue data: not found.

## 4. UK/EU evidence-based players: NICE, NHS Talking Therapies, German DiGA

### Takeaway
The UK and Germany offer formal reimbursement routes that the US lacks. NICE's early value assessment (HTG676) conditionally recommends specific digital CBT products for adult anxiety, including SilverCloud's Space from Anxiety, and NHS England runs digitally enabled therapy (DET) assessment criteria for NHS Talking Therapies. Germany's DiGA directory lists four permanently or currently listed anxiety DiGAs: Invirto, Novego, Selfapy GAD, and velibra. Mindable (panic/agoraphobia) appears to have dropped off.

### Cited Findings
- NICE HTG676, "Digitally enabled therapies for adults with anxiety disorders: early value assessment", covers Space from Anxiety (SilverCloud) for GAD symptoms/unspecified anxiety among others. NHS England says SilverCloud Space from Anxiety (GAD module) is "recommended for use in the NHS while further evidence is generated" — [NICE HTG676](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg676/resources/digitally-enabled-therapies-for-adults-with-anxiety-disorders-early-value-assessment-pdf-1809598771294405); [NHS England DET criteria](https://www.england.nhs.uk/mental-health/adults/nhs-talking-therapies/digital/assessment-criteria/)
- Products covered by NICE's depression or anxiety EVAs are not separately assessed under NHS England's DET Assessment Criteria; other products seeking a Talking Therapies route go through the DET criteria — [NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/mental-health/adults/nhs-talking-therapies/digital/assessment-criteria/)
- Children and young people: NICE HTE3 (its first EVA) recommends guided self-help digital CBT for 5–18-year-olds with mild-to-moderate anxiety or low mood. It covers five technologies, including SilverCloud's Space from Anxiety for Teens, Space from Low Mood for Teens and Space from Low Mood and Anxiety for Teens, all conditional on DTAC approval — [NICE HTE3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/hte3/chapter/1-Recommendations); [NICE news](https://www.nice.org.uk/news/articles/digital-mental-health-tech-for-children-and-young-people-recommended-by-nice-in-first-rapid-healthtech-guidance); [NICE news on CYP anxiety](https://www.nice.org.uk/news/articles/newly-recommended-cbt-therapies-could-help-children-and-young-people-deal-with-anxiety)
- A related NICE guided self-help digital CBT guidance (HTG660 consultation) exists — [NICE HTG660](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg660/documents/consultation-document-2)
- Germany DiGA (anxiety category): the current directory lists Invirto – Die Therapie gegen Angst, Novego: Ängste überwinden, Selfapy's online course for GAD, and velibra — [DiGA-Verzeichnis anxiety](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/digas/psyche/angststoerung)
- velibra is permanently listed, for adults 18+ — [DiGA-Verzeichnis velibra](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/en/diga/velibra). Invirto is permanently listed, for adults 18–65 — [DiGA-Verzeichnis Invirto](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/en/diga/invirto-die-therapie-gegen-angst). Two anxiety apps moved to permanent reimbursability in January 2023 — [DAZ](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2023/01/19/zwei-apps-bei-angststoerungen-nun-dauerhaft-erstattungsfaehig); [PTAheute](https://www.ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2023/01/16/zwei-apps-bei-angststoerungen-nun-dauerhaft-erstattungsfaehig)
- Mindable (panic disorder & agoraphobia) was provisionally listed (vorläufig) as of 2023 sources but is absent from the current anxiety-category list — [PTAheute Apr 2023](https://www.ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2023/04/18/neue-diga-bei-borderline-tabakkonsum-und-aengsten); [Deutsche Angst-Hilfe](https://www.angstselbsthilfe.de/wissen/therapieformen-bei-angststoerungen/digitale-gesundheitsanwendungen-apps/)
- Provisional DiGA listing lasts 12 months while evidence is generated — [Deutsche Angst-Hilfe](https://www.angstselbsthilfe.de/wissen/therapieformen-bei-angststoerungen/digitale-gesundheitsanwendungen-apps/)
- Methodological quality of permanently listed DiGA studies has been criticized — [ScienceDirect (ZEFQ)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1865921722001763)

### Inferences
- In the UK, SilverCloud (Amwell) is the entrenched incumbent for GAD inside NHS Talking Therapies. A new entrant would need NICE EVA inclusion or DET criteria plus DTAC, and in practice to win local commissioning, which is a long sales cycle.
- Germany's anxiety DiGA field is small (≈4 products) but already covers GAD and mixed anxiety. Mindable's apparent exit suggests panic-specific products struggle to prove "positive care effects" within the provisional window, which is both a warning and a sign of a gap in panic.

### Gaps
- Minddistrict's NHS status was not found in results.
- The current HTG676 list of all included technologies, and its final versus draft status and date, are not confirmed. The PDF was not fetched.
- Mindable's delisting is unconfirmed (inferred from its absence), and the date and reason are unknown.
- DiGA prices and prescription volumes for anxiety DiGAs (BfArM / GKV-Spitzenverband reports) were not retrieved.
- France (PECAN), Belgium (mHealthBelgium) and other EU reimbursement routes were not researched.

## 5. Consolidation, layoffs, shutdowns, funding trends 2022–2026

### Takeaway
Overall US digital health funding recovered to USD 14.2B in 2025 (+35% YoY, the highest since 2022), but it is concentrated in AI (54% of dollars) and mega-rounds. Mental health has been hit by a series of down-round exits (Headspace, Talkspace), consumer retreats (Woebot), restructurings (Headspace in 2023 and 2024), and federal enforcement against telehealth stimulant prescribers (Cerebral, Done). Capital is available for AI-native mental health (Slingshot USD 93M) but not for undifferentiated consumer apps.

### Cited Findings

## 6. White space for a new evidence-based anxiety app

### Takeaway
Based on the evidence gathered (much of it inferential), the crowded zones are general wellness/meditation, DTC therapy marketplaces, general AI chat, and GAD-focused digital CBT, where DaylightRx (US), SilverCloud (UK) and four DiGAs (DE) already sit. The thinnest-served areas appear to be condition-specific protocols beyond GAD (panic, social anxiety, health anxiety), regulated and clinician-integrated AI with measurement-based care, and underserved populations. This conclusion comes mainly from what the incumbent lists do not include, not from direct market data.

### Cited Findings
- The cleared/recommended incumbents are GAD-centric: DaylightRx is indicated for GAD — [Big Health](https://www.bighealth.com/news/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-daylightrx); the NHS-recommended Space from Anxiety is the GAD module — [NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/mental-health/adults/nhs-talking-therapies/digital/assessment-criteria/); the German DiGAs include a GAD-specific course (Selfapy) and general anxiety programs — [DiGA-Verzeichnis](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/digas/psyche/angststoerung)
- The panic-specific DiGA (Mindable) appears to have dropped out of the current listing — [DiGA-Verzeichnis](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/digas/psyche/angststoerung) vs [PTAheute 2023](https://www.ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2023/04/18/neue-diga-bei-borderline-tabakkonsum-und-aengsten)
- Youth: NICE created a specific guided self-help pathway for ages 5–18 that requires DTAC — [NICE HTE3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/hte3/chapter/1-Recommendations). Pediatric mental health still attracts funding (Handspring USD 12M, H1 2025) — [Rock Health](https://rockhealth.com/rock-weekly/2025-half-year-funding-6-4b-across-245-deals/). Invirto's DiGA label excludes over-65s — [DiGA-Verzeichnis Invirto](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/en/diga/invirto-die-therapie-gegen-angst)
- Safety gap in general LLMs: >1M weekly ChatGPT users show suicidal intent indicators, and critics note there is no evidence that users get to help faster — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/openai-says-over-a-million-people-talk-to-chatgpt-about-suicide-weekly); [Platformer](https://www.platformer.news/openai-mental-health-research-chatgpt-suicide-delusions/)
- Regulatory vacuum for LLM tools: Woebot's founder cited the lack of FDA guidance for LLM-based tools as a reason to exit — [Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/ai-psychotherapy-shutdown-what-woebots-exit-signals-for-clinicians/)
- Buyer demand is for unified employer/payer mental-health platforms with clinician networks (the Sword–Headspace rationale) — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/09/16/sword-acquires-headspace-300m-all-cash-ai-care-mental-health-platform/)
- Payer channel growth: BetterHelp is building insurance revenue (≈USD 28–40M run rate) as its cash-pay DTC business shrinks — [StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Board+Changes/Pineal+Capital+pushes+TELADOC+for+buybacks+and+strategic+review/26249709.html)

### Inferences
- **Subtype white space.** Panic disorder, social anxiety disorder and health anxiety have strong CBT evidence bases (exposure, interoceptive exposure, internet CBT). In the regulated and reimbursed tier, no dominant cleared or recommended product turned up for them in this research. Consumer panic apps (Rootd, DARE) exist but were not verified as clinically validated. OCD is served by NOCD (ERP therapy network), not verified in this session.
- **Clinician-integrated / blended care.** Payers and employers are buying platforms with clinician networks. A protocolized anxiety module with measurement-based care (GAD-7/PDSS/SPIN tracking) that plugs into such platforms, or into NHS Talking Therapies via DET criteria, may be more viable than a standalone DTC app.
- **Safe-AI positioning.** Between unregulated LLMs (huge reach, safety concerns) and retreating regulated chatbots (Woebot), there is a gap for a protocol-bound, clinically governed AI CBT tool with escalation. Wysa occupies part of it (B2B, NHS) and Slingshot is well funded, so the gap is not empty.
- **Populations.** Evidence and labels skew to working-age adults (DaylightRx 22+, Invirto 18–65). Older adults, perinatal anxiety, adolescents in the US (UK has the NICE HTE3 route), men, and non-English speakers appear less directly served by cleared or listed products. This is an inference from the indication lists, not from direct gap analyses.
- **Channel.** DTC consumer is saturated and low-WTP (Calm about USD 70/yr, BetterHelp declining). Employer/payer B2B, UK NHS commissioning and German DiGA reimbursement are the realistic paths for an evidence-based entrant. Each requires RCT-grade evidence and long sales cycles.

### Gaps
- No direct studies or market data quantify unmet demand by anxiety subtype or population.
- NOCD (OCD/ERP; background knowledge says it is venture-backed and in-network with insurers, unverified), perinatal-anxiety apps, older-adult apps, and non-English anxiety apps were not searched.
- The employer-channel competitive set (Lyra, Spring Health, Modern Health, Calm Health, Headspace/Sword) was only partially covered; their user numbers and pricing were not retrieved.
- The adoption and reimbursement status of CMS DMHT codes in the US is unknown from this research.
