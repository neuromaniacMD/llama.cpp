# Clinical evidence base for app-delivered anxiety interventions (as of Nov 2026)

Research note: compiled from web search results (search snippets plus abstracts and mirrors). Direct fetches of pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and c4tbh.org were blocked in this session, so several figures come from abstracts or secondary write-ups and should be checked against full text before external publication. These cases are flagged below.

## 1. What do meta-analyses say about smartphone/digital interventions for anxiety (effect sizes, controls, guidance, durability)?

### Takeaway
Standalone smartphone apps for anxiety reliably give small effects, roughly g = 0.25-0.33 against controls overall. These fall to about g = 0.1-0.2 against active controls and rise to about g = 0.45 against waitlist. Guided internet CBT programs (often web-based, multi-module) give much larger effects (g around 0.8), so "app" and "digital CBT" evidence should not be conflated. Umbrella-level grading (Goldberg 2022) finds little "convincing" evidence, and effects shrink as comparators become more rigorous.

### Cited Findings
- **Firth et al. 2017 (J Affect Disord 218:15-22):** In the first meta-analysis of smartphone RCTs for anxiety, apps outperformed controls on total anxiety, g = 0.325 (95% CI 0.17-0.48), with no evidence of publication bias. — [Western Sydney Univ repository](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/can-smartphone-mental-health-interventions-reduce-symptoms-of-anx/); [Univ. of Manchester news](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/discover/news/smartphones-new-weapon-in-war-against-anxiety/)
- **Firth 2017, by comparator:** g = 0.45 (95% CI 0.30-0.61) vs waitlist or inactive controls, and g = 0.19 (95% CI 0.07-0.31) vs active controls. The authors said it "has yet to be established" whether apps can match recognised anxiety treatments. — [Western Sydney Univ repository](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/can-smartphone-mental-health-interventions-reduce-symptoms-of-anx/)
- **Linardon et al. 2019 (World Psychiatry):** reported g = 0.30 for generalized anxiety across 28 trials. This figure comes from a secondary summary. — [Mental Elf](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/mental-health/depression/apps-for-depression-and-anxiety-big-new-meta-analysis-supports-effectiveness/)
- **Linardon et al. 2024 (World Psychiatry 23(1):139-149), 176 RCTs of apps for depression and anxiety:**
  - Generalized anxiety: g = 0.26 (95% CI 0.21-0.31), NNT about 12.4, with significant heterogeneity. — [Mental Elf](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/mental-health/depression/apps-for-depression-and-anxiety-big-new-meta-analysis-supports-effectiveness/); [VU Amsterdam record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/current-evidence-on-the-efficacy-of-mental-health-smartphoneapps-/)
  - Subtypes: social anxiety g = 0.52 and OCD symptoms g = 0.51 (moderate); PTSD g = 0.12 (small); panic g = -0.12 (non-significant). The authors caution that most trials had high risk of bias and small samples. — [Western Sydney Univ record](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/current-evidence-on-the-efficacy-of-mental-health-smartphoneapps-/)
  - Moderators: anxiety effects were larger when generalized anxiety was the primary target and when the app was CBT-based or had mood monitoring. Mindfulness and cognitive-training apps had smaller effects. — [Western Sydney Univ record](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/current-evidence-on-the-efficacy-of-mental-health-smartphoneapps-/); [Simply Psychology summary](https://www.simplypsychology.org/?p=55767)
  - Inactive controls gave larger effects, which the authors interpret as a possible "digital placebo" effect. — [Mental Elf](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/mental-health/depression/apps-for-depression-and-anxiety-big-new-meta-analysis-supports-effectiveness/)
- **Goldberg et al. 2022 (PLOS Digital Health 1(1):e0000002), umbrella meta-review:** 14 meta-analyses, 145 RCTs and 47,940 participants. The authors "failed to find convincing evidence" of efficacy for any outcome and comparison combination.
  - Grading of effect sizes: 8 highly suggestive/promising, 4 suggestive, 14 weak and 8 non-significant.
  - Effects and evidence strength diminished as comparators became more rigorous.
  - No moderators were identified, and adverse effects were not reported.
  - Sources: [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/2f1575c0f89c494fb5d8e89059ed1443); [STAT News 2022](https://www.statnews.com/2022/01/19/mental-health-meditation-app-evidence/); [Mad in America summary](https://www.madinamerica.com/?p=230621)
- Goldberg told STAT that phone-based interventions "might not be uniquely effective" but are effective relative to nothing or to non-therapeutic interventions. — [STAT News 2022](https://www.statnews.com/2022/01/19/mental-health-meditation-app-evidence/)
- **Guided vs unguided, Pauley et al. 2021 (Psychological Medicine):** 47 RCTs, 53 comparisons and 4,958 participants with anxiety disorders. Pooled g = 0.80 (95% CI 0.68-0.93); guided g = 0.84 vs unguided g = 0.64, with no significant difference. This review covers broader digital (largely internet) interventions, not just phone apps. — [Pauley et al. PDF](https://mhpsshub.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Pauly_Anxiety.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291721001999)
- **Guided iCBT in routine care, Etzelmueller et al. 2020 (JMIR):** 19 studies and 30 groups, using uncontrolled pre-post designs. The pooled within-group effect was g = 0.94 for anxiety studies (1.78 for depression), and average deterioration was 2.9%. — [JMIR 2020](https://www.jmir.org/2020/8/e18100)

### Inferences
- A realistic claim for a standalone, unguided anxiety app is a small effect (g about 0.2-0.3) vs mostly inactive controls, and probably g at or below 0.2 vs a credible active or sham control. Effects near g = 0.8 belong to guided, structured iCBT programs for diagnosed anxiety disorders, not to self-help apps.
- The Firth 2017 -> Linardon 2019 -> Linardon 2024 sequence is roughly 0.33 -> 0.30 -> 0.26. As the field grew, pooled estimates drifted downward, which is a typical sign that early small trials overestimated effects.
- Condition targeting matters. Apps aimed at social anxiety and OCD show moderate effects, while panic-focused apps show none at the pooled level (though device-based breathing for panic shows promise; see section 2).
- Human guidance probably helps more with engagement and retention (see section 4) than with effect size per completer. In Pauley 2021 the guided vs unguided difference was not significant.

### Gaps
- **Weisel et al. 2019 (npj Digital Medicine, standalone apps):** exact anxiety effect sizes were not retrieved in this session, so no number is reported here.
- **Durability:** I found no pooled estimate of long-term (6-12 month) follow-up effects for anxiety apps. Linardon 2024 follow-up analyses were not retrieved (PMC was blocked).
- **Cochrane:** no anxiety-app Cochrane review was located in this search.
- **Confidence intervals:** the Linardon 2024 figures for the subtypes and for active vs inactive controls come from abstracts and summaries. Verify them in the full text.

## 2. Which therapeutic modalities have the strongest digital evidence for anxiety?

### Takeaway
CBT-based content (including exposure-based components for social anxiety and OCD) has the strongest and most consistent digital evidence. Mindfulness apps give small effects (g about 0.28) that drop to about g = 0.10 against active comparators. Attention bias modification (ABM) is the cautionary example: early medium effects shrank to small or non-significant ones in larger meta-analyses. Capnometry-guided breathing (Freespira) has FDA clearance and high reported remission in panic disorder, but its evidence is mostly uncontrolled and sponsor-run.

### Cited Findings
- **CBT apps:** in Linardon 2024, anxiety effect sizes were significantly larger for CBT apps and apps with mood monitoring. — [Western Sydney Univ record](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/current-evidence-on-the-efficacy-of-mental-health-smartphoneapps-/)
- **Mindfulness apps, Linardon et al. 2023 (Clinical Psychology Review):**
  - 45 RCTs; anxiety g = 0.28 (95% CI 0.21-0.35; 48 comparisons; N = 6,082; NNT = 11.5) vs controls. — [Linardon et al. 2023 PDF, Center for Healthy Minds](https://centerhealthyminds.org/assets/files-publications/Linardon-et-al-2023-The-efficacy-of-mindfulness-apps-on-symptoms-of-depression-and-anxiety-An-updated-meta-analysis-of-randomized-controlled-trials.pdf)
  - Against active therapeutic comparisons, the anxiety effect was g = 0.10, from few studies. The preprint wording on significance is garbled, so verify it in the full text. — [same source](https://centerhealthyminds.org/assets/files-publications/Linardon-et-al-2023-The-efficacy-of-mindfulness-apps-on-symptoms-of-depression-and-anxiety-An-updated-meta-analysis-of-randomized-controlled-trials.pdf)
  - Prior estimates cited in that paper: g about 0.28 (Gal et al. 2021) and g = 0.08 (Tan et al. 2022). — [same source](https://centerhealthyminds.org/assets/files-publications/Linardon-et-al-2023-The-efficacy-of-mindfulness-apps-on-symptoms-of-depression-and-anxiety-An-updated-meta-analysis-of-randomized-controlled-trials.pdf)
- **Mindfulness market leaders:** Headspace had 14 RCTs identified, only 5 of 14 (36%) preregistered; Calm had 1 RCT. — [Longyear and Kushlev analysis (preprint/Substack)](https://longyearhealth.substack.com/p/do-all-those-mental-health-apps-actually)
- **Attention bias modification / cognitive bias modification:**
  - Hakamata et al. 2010 reported a medium ABM effect on anxiety, d = 0.61, while calling for replication. — [Univ. of Minnesota record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/attention-bias-modification-treatment-a-meta-analysis-toward-the-/)
  - Hallion and Ruscio's later meta-analysis found only g = 0.13 for anxiety and depression combined (cited via the Cristea literature). — [Cristea et al. 2015, Br J Psychiatry](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/efficacy-of-cognitive-bias-modification-interventions-in-anxietyand-depression-metaanalysis/F277CBE661CF8960AF9C2014B42300AB)
  - Cristea, Kok and Cuijpers 2015 (49 trials) found effects "small considering all the samples, and mostly non-significant for patient samples". — [Br J Psychiatry](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/efficacy-of-cognitive-bias-modification-interventions-in-anxietyand-depression-metaanalysis/F277CBE661CF8960AF9C2014B42300AB)
  - Cristea's 2020 network meta-analysis: interpretation bias modification beat waitlist and sham, but ABM benefits appeared only in post-hoc sensitivity analyses that excluded PTSD trials. Prediction intervals included SMD = 0. — [Univ. of Pavia record](https://iris.unipv.it/handle/11571/1343097)
- **Freespira (capnometry-guided respiratory intervention, CGRI) for panic disorder:**
  - FDA cleared it for adults in 2013. — [MDIC](https://mdic.org/?p=11623)
  - Multisite benchmarking trial (2017): 69 adults in 4 non-academic clinics, 4 weeks of treatment. Intent-to-treat response was 83% and remission 54%; 56 of 69 completed. Among completers, remission was about 53% at 2 months and about 70% at 12 months. The trial was uncontrolled and developer-associated. — [PMC5344940](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5344940/)
  - A 2022 real-world cohort of 1,395 patients reported a 93% response rate (40% or more drop in PDSS) at 1 year. — [PMC9712796](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9712796)
  - May 2025: 510(k) clearance K233337 for ages 13-17. The company reports a 54% mean PDSS-A reduction, 68% clinically meaningful improvement and 45% remission (company press release figures). — [BusinessWire 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250513424670/en/Freespira-Receives-FDA-Clearance-for-Adolescents-Ages-1317-Clinically-Validated-At-Home-Treatment-Reduces-Panic-Disorder-Symptom-Severity-by-54-and-PTSD-Symptom-Severity-by-44); [GeekWire 2025](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/seattle-area-company-lands-fda-approval-for-drug-free-treatment-to-address-teen-anxiety/)
- **Digital CBT for GAD (DaylightRx, Big Health):** see section 5. An RCT of 256 participants with moderate-to-severe GAD against waitlist was published in Depression and Anxiety in 2020 (Carl et al., doi:10.1002/da.23079). — [Psychiatric Times 2024](https://psychiatrictimes.com/view/first-digital-treatment-for-generalized-anxiety-disorder-cleared-by-the-fda)

### Inferences
- **Strongest bet for a new entrant:** structured CBT with exposure elements, targeted at a defined condition (GAD, social anxiety), with symptom or mood monitoring. This is the profile behind the larger pooled effects and behind the FDA-cleared and NICE-recommended products.
- **Mindfulness and relaxation:** they are defensible as adjuncts, but the evidence does not support marketing them as treatment equivalents. Against active comparators the effect is near zero to small.
- **ABM:** building a product on ABM carries scientific risk because its replication record is poor. Interpretation bias modification looks somewhat better.
- **Breathing and biofeedback:** a hardware-assisted approach (capnometry) has the strongest "device" story for panic, but its evidence is largely open-label. A plain breathing app without CO2 feedback cannot borrow Freespira's evidence.

### Gaps
- **ACT and applied relaxation:** I found no app-specific pooled effect sizes for anxiety in this search window. ACT apps were not covered by the retrieved results.
- **Exposure as a standalone app component:** no app-level pooled estimate was found. The evidence comes indirectly from iCBT and from the social anxiety and OCD subgroups.
- **Biofeedback (HRV) apps:** no meta-analytic figures were retrieved.

## 3. What proportion of commercially available anxiety apps are evidence-based or have published RCTs? Evaluation frameworks

### Takeaway
Only a small minority of available anxiety and mental health apps have any published efficacy evidence, roughly 2-25% depending on the sample and the definition (feasibility study vs efficacy trial). Claims of being "evidence-based" in app store copy far exceed the actual evidence. Curated frameworks (MIND/mindapps.org with the APA model, NICE, ORCHA, PsyberGuide) exist to separate the two.

### Cited Findings
- **Marshall et al. 2019, "The Digital Psychiatrist":** a systematic store search found 293 anxiety/depression therapy apps. 162 (55.3%) claimed an evidence-based framework, but only 10 of those 162 (6.2%) had published efficacy evidence, about 3.4% of all 293. — [PMC6872533](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6872533/)
- **Larsen et al. 2019 (npj Digital Medicine), as reported:** 64% of mental health apps claimed efficacy but only 14% described any evidence. In a 73-app subset, only 2 cited direct evidence from research on the app itself. — [MedCity News 2019](https://medcitynews.com/2019/03/research-shows-most-consumer-behavioral-health-apps-have-no-scientific-backing/)
- **Stress and wellness apps (Lau et al. 2020, JMIR Mental Health):** only 2.08% (21 of 1,009) had published peer-reviewed evidence of feasibility and/or efficacy. — [JMIR Mental Health 2020](https://mental.jmir.org/2020/6/e16525/XML)
- **MIND database / mindapps.org (Torous lab, built on the APA App Evaluation Model):** the team has evaluated more than 500 apps. — [digitalpsych.org](https://www.digitalpsych.org/mindapps.html)
  - A 2021 analysis of 278 apps found 44 (<25%) supported by a feasibility or efficacy study.
  - Of the same 278, 238 had a privacy policy, typically at a very high reading level.
  - Marketplace metrics (stars, downloads) did not track clinical utility.
  - Sources: [Mad in America 2021](https://www.madinamerica.com/2021/04/mental-health-app-marketplace-mess-researchers-find/); [PMC7986656 PDF](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/bb558c3f6ee872c8e938ee1fa13c148b/PMC7986656.pdf)
- **NHS Health Apps Library:** only 15% of apps reportedly provided evidence. This is secondary and unverified against the NHS source. — [Longyear and Kushlev preprint](https://www.scribd.com/document/648787196/Longyear-Kushlev-Preprint)
- **NICE HTE9 / HTG676 (Feb-May 2023 early value assessment):** NICE conditionally recommended digitally enabled therapies for anxiety. For GAD and unspecified anxiety the recommended product was SilverCloud "Space from Anxiety".
  - Use requires support from a psychological wellbeing practitioner or high-intensity therapist, CE/UKCA marking, DTAC compliance and an NHS Talking Therapies assessment.
  - NICE judged the evidence base "limited for all of them", with preliminary indications of cost-effectiveness, and set a review within 4 years.
  - Sources: [NICE HTG676 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg676/chapter/recommendations); [NICE HTE9 committee discussion](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/HTE9/chapter/3-committee-discussion); [UKAuthority](https://ukauthority.com/articles/nice-conditionally-backs-digital-therapies-for-treating-depression-and-anxiety/)
  - A Wired-Gov reprint lists products differently from the NICE committee text; treat the NICE pages as authoritative. — [Wired-Gov](https://www.wired-gov.net/wg/news.nsf/articles/Eight+digitally+enabled+therapies+to+treat+depression+and+anxiety+in+adults+conditionally+recommended+by+NICE+01032023132000)

### Inferences
- A new entrant with even one well-conducted, preregistered RCT would place in roughly the top 5% of anxiety apps on evidence. The bar for differentiation is low, but the bar for credible claims (active control, preregistration, diagnosed sample) is much higher.
- **UK route:** the NICE/NHS Talking Therapies pathway rewards guided, supported delivery with UKCA marking and DTAC compliance. This shapes the product design (therapist-supported model) as well as the evidence.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 updated census of the proportion of anxiety apps with RCTs was found. Most figures date from 2019-2021.
- No ORCHA report statistics or One Mind PsyberGuide figures were retrieved in this search window.
- No information was retrieved on the 2025 Dwyer et al. paper (Evidence-Based Mental Health, Torous co-author) about MIND.

## 4. Engagement, the "digital placebo" problem, adverse effects/deterioration, and dropout

### Takeaway
In trials, uptake is high (about 92%) but sustained adherence is about 62%, and attrition is about 19% at post-test and about 28% at follow-up. Real-world engagement is far lower. Inactive comparators inflate effects (the "digital placebo"), so credible evidence needs active or sham controls. Deterioration under guided iCBT is low (about 3%), but adverse events are rarely reported in app trials.

### Cited Findings
- **Liu, Torous et al. 2025 (JAMA Psychiatry, published Nov 19 2025, doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2025.3439):** 79 RCTs of depression and anxiety apps.
  - Uptake was about 92% (56 app conditions) and adherence about 62% (21 conditions).
  - Attrition was about 18.6% at post-test and about 28.4% at follow-up.
  - Attrition was lower with reminders or human support and in apps without gamification.
  - App arms had higher attrition than waitlist (RR 1.49, but prediction interval 0.34-6.48), with no meaningful difference vs active controls.
  - For comparison, attrition is about 17% in face-to-face psychotherapy trials and 15-30% in pharmacotherapy trials.
  - Sources: [AJMC Nov 2025](https://www.ajmc.com/view/addressing-uptake-adherence-and-attrition-in-mental-health-apps); [HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/mental-health-apps-gain-high-uptake-struggle-adherence-retention); [Psychiatric News Alert](https://alert.psychnews.org/smartphone-apps-for-anxiety-depression-show-high-initiation-moderate-engagement)
  - Some summaries give slightly different figures (94% uptake; 17%/27% attrition). — [same sources]
- **Torous et al. 2020 (J Affect Disord), depression-app RCTs:** 18 RCTs and N = 3,336. Pooled dropout was 26.2%, rising to 47.8% after adjustment for publication bias. Dropout was lower with human feedback (about 11.7% vs 34.0%) and with in-app mood monitoring. — [PubMed 31969272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31969272/); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/meta-analysis-finds-high-dropout-rates-studies-depression-apps)
- **Linardon 2024:** reported overall dropout of about 24%, similar to face-to-face therapy. — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/addressing-uptake-adherence-and-attrition-in-mental-health-apps); [Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/?p=55767)
- **Digital placebo:** effects were larger against inactive controls in Linardon 2024. — [Mental Elf](https://www.nationalelfservice.net/mental-health/depression/apps-for-depression-and-anxiety-big-new-meta-analysis-supports-effectiveness/)
  - Firth 2017: waitlist g = 0.45 vs active g = 0.19. — [Western Sydney Univ](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/can-smartphone-mental-health-interventions-reduce-symptoms-of-anx/)
  - Goldberg 2022: strength of evidence fell with more rigorous comparators. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2f1575c0f89c494fb5d8e89059ed1443)
- **Adverse effects:** the Goldberg 2022 umbrella review noted that adverse effects were not reported in the included meta-analyses. — [Mad in America summary](https://www.madinamerica.com/?p=230621)
  - Average deterioration in guided iCBT routine care was 2.9% (Etzelmueller 2020). — [JMIR 2020](https://www.jmir.org/2020/8/e18100)
- **Engagement decline in generative AI chatbot studies:** a July 2026 npj Digital Medicine scoping review of 21 studies found that engagement commonly declined over time. — [LetsDataScience summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/review-finds-mental-health-chatbot-safety-evidence-lags-6f451028)

### Inferences
- Trial-level retention (about 70-80%) overstates what a commercial app will see. Plan for real-world retention well below trial figures, and treat engagement design (reminders, human support, check-ins) as part of the "evidence-based" claim.
- Gamification was associated with higher attrition in Liu 2025. This counters a common product assumption and is worth flagging to the product team.
- Payers and regulators increasingly expect sham or active controls (Rejoyn used a sham; see section 5). A waitlist-controlled trial may be enough for publication but weak for differentiation.

### Gaps
- **Real-world retention:** no up-to-date figure was retrieved for anxiety apps specifically (for example, Baumel 2019-style 15- or 30-day retention). Older work suggests very low 30-day retention, but it was not verified here.
- **Deterioration:** no app-specific (vs iCBT) deterioration rate for anxiety was found. Rozental/Ebert negative-effects analyses were not retrieved.

## 5. What it takes to build an evidence base: pilots, RCT cost/timelines, partnerships, regulatory examples

### Takeaway
Regulated precedents show the scale needed. DaylightRx's FDA clearance (Sept 2024) rested on an RCT of 256 participants against waitlist plus supporting trials. Rejoyn's pivotal trial (depression) enrolled 373 participants with a sham control. Wysa's 2022 Breakthrough designation rested on a peer-reviewed RCT in chronic pain patients with comorbid depression and anxiety, but designation is not clearance. I found no reliable public dollar figures for pivotal digital mental health RCTs.

### Cited Findings
- **DaylightRx (Big Health):** FDA-cleared September 4, 2024 as the first digital therapeutic for GAD. It is an adjunct to usual care for adults aged 22 and over, with a 90-day treatment course. — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-daylightrx); [Drug Topics](https://drugtopics.com/view/fda-clears-first-digital-therapeutic-for-treatment-of-generalized-anxiety-disorder); [Big Health](https://www.bighealth.com/news/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-daylightrx)
  - Key trial: Carl et al. 2020 (Depression and Anxiety), a superiority RCT of 256 participants with moderate-to-severe GAD against waitlist. — [Psychiatric Times 2024](https://psychiatrictimes.com/view/first-digital-treatment-for-generalized-anxiety-disorder-cleared-by-the-fda)
  - Reported remission figures above 70% come from company materials. — [Drug Topics](https://drugtopics.com/view/fda-clears-first-digital-therapeutic-for-treatment-of-generalized-anxiety-disorder)
  - Note: the RCT was published in 2020 and clearance came in 2024, about a 4-year gap between the key publication and authorization.
- **Rejoyn (Otsuka/Click, depression) as a sizing benchmark:** the Mirai pivotal trial had a safety sample of 373 (187 Rejoyn vs 186 sham). It was a decentralized trial, and the company would not give a firm timeline. — [Innolitics 510k K231209](https://510k.innolitics.com/device/K231209); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/otsuka-click-therapeutics-kick-decentralized-pivotal-trial-depression-digital-therapeutics)
- **Wysa:** FDA Breakthrough Device Designation in May 2022 for adults with chronic musculoskeletal pain (more than 3 months) plus depression and anxiety. — [Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/psychiatry/20220513/fda-designates-aibased-therapy-for-pain-depression-anxiety-as-breakthrough-device); [Becker's](https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/behavioral-health-technology/mental-health-ai-device-receives-fda-breakthrough-designation.html)
  - Supporting evidence: a peer-reviewed JMIR-family RCT in orthopedic patients over 2 months, reported as more effective than standard orthopedic care and comparable to in-person counseling. — [Voicebot](https://voicebot.ai/2022/05/27/conversational-ai-therapy-startup-wysa-earns/); [Wysa clinical evidence](https://www.wysa.com/clinical-evidence)
  - Designation speeds FDA interaction but is not a marketing authorization. — [FDAnews](https://fdanews.com/articles/207902-wysas-ai-mental-health-software-designated-an-fda-breakthrough-device)
- **Freespira:** it had a 2013 adult clearance and a 2025 adolescent 510(k) (K233337) after trials of a 28-day regimen. Its market-access strategy is discussed by MDIC. — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250513424670/en); [MDIC](https://mdic.org/?p=11623)
- **NICE (UK) route:** conditional recommendation within an Early Value Assessment, with requirements to generate further evidence and a review within 4 years. — [NICE HTG676](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg676/chapter/recommendations)
- **Timeline risk:** RCTs that test apps "as is" can span years, leaving technology obsolete before impact. — [Applied Clinical Trials](https://appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/digital-therapeutics-and-decentralized-trials-a-match-made-in-clinical)
- **Evidence guidance:** the DTx Alliance publishes clinical evidence recommendations for digital therapeutics. — [DTx Alliance 2023 PDF](https://dtxalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/DTx-Clincial-Evidence-Recommendations.pdf)
- **Funding scale (dated):** Limbix raised $9M (2021) to develop a teen depression prescription digital therapeutic. — [search result via MobiHealthNews/Applied Clinical Trials context](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/otsuka-click-therapeutics-kick-decentralized-pivotal-trial-depression-digital-therapeutics)
- **Payer perspective:** payer views on prescription digital therapeutics for psychiatric disorders are discussed in AJMC (Oct 2025), indicating that reimbursement depends on robust evidence. — [AJMC Oct 2025](https://www.ajmc.com/view/payer-perspectives-on-prescription-digital-therapeutics-for-psychiatric-disorders)

### Inferences
These are rough planning estimates. They are not sourced dollar figures; see Gaps.

- **A plausible evidence ladder for a new entrant:**
  1. Usability/feasibility pilot, n around 30-60, single-arm, months.
  2. Waitlist- or attention-controlled RCT, n around 200-300 (similar to the DaylightRx trial), often run with an academic partner who can publish.
  3. A pivotal RCT with an active or sham comparator, n around 300-400 (similar to Rejoyn), if seeking FDA clearance or payer coverage.
  4. Ongoing real-world evidence (registries, NHS Talking Therapies outcome data).
- **Time from first RCT to regulatory authorization:** the DaylightRx precedent suggests several years. A wellness-positioned (non-device) app can make limited claims after step 2, but "treats anxiety" claims in the US imply FDA oversight.
- **Academic partnership** (for example, university-run trials, as with Therabot at Dartmouth) lowers direct cost and raises credibility but slows timelines and cedes some control over publication.

## 6. Role of AI/LLM chatbots in anxiety treatment: Therabot and other trials; safety

### Takeaway
The first RCT of a fully generative therapy chatbot (Therabot, NEJM AI, March 2025) showed significant symptom reductions vs waitlist for GAD, depression and eating-disorder risk over 8 weeks. Safety still required human oversight: staff intervened 15 times for safety concerns and 13 times to correct inappropriate responses. Pooled evidence for generative AI chatbots shows small-to-moderate effects on general distress (SMD about 0.3-0.36), but with few, short trials and no standardized safety evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- **Therabot design:** Heinz et al., NEJM AI, March 27, 2025 (Dartmouth). N = 210 adults with depression, GAD or high eating-disorder risk, randomized to Therabot vs waitlist, with outcomes at 4 and 8 weeks. — [Dartmouth TTO 2025](https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250327/AI-powered-therapy-chatbot-shows-significant-mental-health-benefits.aspx)
- **Therabot results:**
  - The GAD group showed an average 31% reduction in anxiety symptoms, with many moving from moderate to mild or below the clinical threshold. All three symptom domains improved significantly more than waitlist. — [Dartmouth TTO 2025](https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits)
  - Participants averaged about 6 hours of use, described as about eight 45-minute therapy sessions. — [Dartmouth TTO 2025](https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/03/28/1114001/the-first-trial-of-generative-ai-therapy-shows-it-might-help-with-depression/amp/)
- **Therabot safety:** staff intervened 15 times for safety concerns (including suicidal ideation) and 13 times to correct inappropriate responses such as medical advice. — [JMIR 2025 meta-analysis citing Therabot](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e78238/PDF)
  - Lead author Nicholas Jacobson: "no generative AI agent is ready to operate fully autonomously in mental health". — [Dartmouth TTO 2025](https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits)
  - Dartmouth recognized the trial again in July 2026. — [Dartmouth TTO 2026](https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2026/07/nicholas-jacobson-recognized-foundational-therabot-trial)
- **Therabot reporting discrepancies:** secondary sources differ on sample details (one Psychology Today piece says 106) and on how the control arm is described. The 210 total from Dartmouth is authoritative. — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/urban-survival/202504/ai-therapy-breakthrough-new-study-reveals-promising-results)
- **Generative AI chatbot meta-analysis (JMIR 2025, e78238):** a modest, significant reduction in negative mental health outcomes, effect size about 0.30-0.36 depending on version (one report gives 95% CI 0.004-0.59), from about 12-14 RCTs. The evidence is skewed toward non-clinical, older and female samples. — [JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025//e78238/); [PMC12707440](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12707440/); [LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/genai-chatbots-reduce-negative-mental-health-outcomes-2a88f21e)
- **Youth and young adult chatbot meta-analysis (JMIR 2025, e79850):** 31 RCTs and N = 29,637. Distress fell by SMD -0.35 (95% CI -0.46 to -0.24), and the authors called for safety protocols. — [JMIR 2025 e79850](https://www.jmir.org/2025//e79850/)
- **npj Digital Medicine scoping review (July 2026):** 21 studies of generative AI mental health chatbots. Most were early-stage, engagement declined over time, and evidence on efficacy, long-term use and standardized safety evaluation was limited. — [LetsDataScience summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/review-finds-mental-health-chatbot-safety-evidence-lags-6f451028)
- **Commercial AI chatbots:** a Karger (Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics) article examines commercial AI mental health chatbots as low-threshold interventions. It was not reviewed in detail. — [Karger](https://karger.com/pps/article/doi/10.1159/000552072/948904/Commercial-AI-Based-Mental-Health-Chatbots-as-Low)

### Inferences
- LLM chatbots are the newest and least-proven modality. One positive waitlist-controlled RCT (Therabot) plus small-to-moderate pooled effects is roughly where app CBT stood about a decade ago.
- Therabot's roughly 28 human interventions in about 100 users over 8 weeks suggest that any LLM-based anxiety product needs clinician-in-the-loop monitoring, crisis escalation and output guardrails. These add operating cost and regulatory exposure.
- Therabot's comparator was waitlist, so its effects are probably inflated relative to an active control, consistent with the digital placebo pattern in section 4.

- **Therabot effect sizes:** the exact standardized effect sizes (Cohen's d for GAD-7 at 4 and 8 weeks) were not retrievable in this session (c4tbh.org and PMC blocked). Pull them from the NEJM AI paper.
- No anxiety-specific meta-analysis of generative AI chatbots was found. Pooled estimates are transdiagnostic.
