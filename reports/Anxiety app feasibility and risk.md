# Evidence-based anxiety apps survive only inside institutions

As of November 2026 it is entirely feasible to build an evidence-based anxiety app. It is not feasible to build a standalone consumer anxiety app business. The clinical recipe is well understood: structured CBT with exposure, symptom monitoring and some human support. The regulatory routes are mapped, including an FDA 510(k) predicate chain and Germany's DiGA. Every link that turns a good app into revenue, though, is weak or getting weaker. App stores carry an estimated 10,000–20,000 mental health apps. The two scale tiers (meditation subscriptions and therapy marketplaces) are being sold at a fraction of their 2021 valuations: Headspace went for up to about $300M against a ~$3B peak. ChatGPT now serves as a free, unregulated mental-health channel for hundreds of millions of people each week. Real-world 30-day retention for unguided mental health apps sits around 3%. I rate overall risk as **high** and the risk to the business model as **very high**. Clinical-evidence risk is **moderate**, and regulatory/legal risk ranges from **moderate** for a scripted CBT product to **high** for a generative-AI "therapist". The real white space is narrow but real. It lies in condition-specific protocols beyond generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), above all social anxiety, and in clinically governed modules sold into platforms, payers and national health systems that already own the patient. The most viable paths therefore run B2B2C or through reimbursement. The least viable are a direct-to-consumer subscription app and an autonomous AI chatbot.

## Twenty thousand apps, but only a handful get paid to treat anxiety

The market looks large and is crowded at the bottom. Analyst firms put global mental health app revenue at about **$8.0–8.5B in 2025**. "Depression and anxiety management" is the largest segment at roughly **$2.2–2.4B** ([GMInsights](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/mental-health-apps-market); [Persistence](https://persistencemarketresearch.com/article/mental-health-apps-2025.asp)). These paywalled estimates draw on shared inputs and define scope inconsistently, and most of that "anxiety" spend is general meditation and wellness, not treatment. App-store counts of roughly **20,000 mental health apps** go back to a 2018 study and have been re-cited ever since; one systematic store search found only 1,435, so the number depends heavily on definitions ([JMIR Human Factors 2026](https://humanfactors.jmir.org/2026/1/e67944); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7801857)). Either way, a long tail fights over a pool of downloads that is shrinking. Appfigures described category demand as "at an all-time low" in May 2024, while Calm and Headspace traded falling downloads for higher prices ([Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20240510?f=3); [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20230106?f=4)).

The incumbents are consolidating at sharply lower valuations. Sword Health agreed to buy Headspace in September 2026, all-cash, for up to **$300M**, about a tenth of its 2021 valuation. The aim was to cross-sell into Headspace's employer and payer base and its network of more than 15,000 clinicians. Post-close layoffs followed in October ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/sword-health-acquire-headspace-all-cash-deal); [Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/headspace-once-worth-3-billion-sells-for-300-million-plus-9-more-m-a-deals/); [BH Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2026/10/16/headspace-issues-layoffs-following-sword-health-acquisition/)). Universal Health Services agreed to buy Talkspace for about **$835M enterprise value**, against a $1.4B SPAC valuation in 2021 ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/talkspace-835-million-acquisition-by-universal-health-services/)). The notes do not confirm whether that deal has closed. Teladoc guided BetterHelp revenue 7–11% lower year on year for Q1 2026 ([StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Board+Changes/Pineal+Capital+pushes+TELADOC+for+buybacks+and+strategic+review/26249709.html)). The pattern is consistent: buyers are diversified care platforms and provider systems that want distribution and clinician networks, not standalone apps.

Evidence-based AI chatbots have pulled back. Woebot retired its consumer app on **June 30, 2025**, and its founder cited the cost of FDA authorization and the lack of FDA guidance for LLM tools ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/woebot-health-shutting-down-its-app); [Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/ai-psychotherapy-shutdown-what-woebots-exit-signals-for-clinicians/)). Wysa now earns more than 80% of its revenue from businesses and health plans ([The Hemingway Report](https://www.thehemingwayreport.com/articles/43-wysa-x-april-deal-breakdown)). The consumer bar is now set by a general-purpose model. OpenAI disclosed about **800M weekly users**, with more than 1M a week showing explicit indicators of suicidal planning ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/openai-says-over-a-million-people-talk-to-chatgpt-about-suicide-weekly)). A new app cannot compete with that on availability or price. It can compete only on what an LLM lacks: structured protocols, measured outcomes, clinician escalation and regulatory standing.

The regulated tier is the opposite of crowded, but it is concentrated on GAD. Big Health's **DaylightRx** became the first FDA-cleared digital treatment for GAD in 2024 ([Big Health](https://www.bighealth.com/news/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-daylightrx)). In England, NICE's early value assessment conditionally recommends SilverCloud's Space from Anxiety (GAD module) together with products including Minddistrict and iCT-SAD ([NICE HTG676](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg676/resources/digitally-enabled-therapies-for-adults-with-anxiety-disorders-early-value-assessment-pdf-1809598771294405)). Germany lists four anxiety DiGAs: Invirto, Novego, Selfapy's GAD course and velibra. Mindable, a panic DiGA, is no longer in the anxiety category ([DiGA-Verzeichnis](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/digas/psyche/angststoerung)). Capital is still available, but it goes to AI. US digital health funding reached **$14.2B in 2025**, and AI companies took **54%** of it ([Rock Health](https://rockhealth.com/rock-weekly/2025-year-end-funding-14-2b-raised-across-482-deals/)). Mental health tech funding was about **$1.4B in 2024, roughly half its 2021 peak** ([ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/mental-health-tech-in-2026-where-the-5b-market-is-consolidating)). Slingshot AI's $93M raise shows that well-funded AI-native rivals already occupy the "safe AI therapy" pitch ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2025/12/08/rock-health-2025-report-mental-health-ai-and-longevity-tech-lead-innovation-curve/)).

## The evidence supports small effects that shrink under honest comparison

The science is not what blocks feasibility, but it caps what a product can honestly claim. The largest meta-analysis of app RCTs (Linardon et al. 2024, 176 trials) found **g = 0.26 for generalized anxiety (NNT ≈ 12)**. Effects were larger for CBT-based apps and apps with mood monitoring, and smaller for mindfulness and cognitive training ([VU Amsterdam](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/current-evidence-on-the-efficacy-of-mental-health-smartphoneapps-/); [Western Sydney Univ](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/current-evidence-on-the-efficacy-of-mental-health-smartphoneapps-/)). Pooled estimates have drifted down as the field matured, from 0.33 (Firth 2017) to 0.30 (Linardon 2019) to 0.26. That is the usual sign that early small trials overstated benefit. The comparator drives the result: Firth found **g = 0.45 against waitlist but 0.19 against active controls** ([Western Sydney Univ](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/can-smartphone-mental-health-interventions-reduce-symptoms-of-anx/)). Goldberg's 2022 umbrella review (145 RCTs, ~48,000 participants) "failed to find convincing evidence" for any outcome–comparator combination ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2f1575c0f89c494fb5d8e89059ed1443)). Effects near **g = 0.8** do exist, but only for structured, largely web-based internet CBT for diagnosed anxiety disorders, guided or unguided ([Pauley et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291721001999)). A self-help app cannot borrow that number.

The subtype breakdown matters for strategy. In Linardon 2024, apps for **social anxiety (g = 0.52)** and **OCD symptoms (g = 0.51)** showed moderate effects. **Panic-focused apps showed none (g = −0.12, non-significant)**, though most trials were small and at high risk of bias ([Western Sydney Univ](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/current-evidence-on-the-efficacy-of-mental-health-smartphoneapps-/)). Mindfulness apps reach g = 0.28 overall but only about **0.10 against active comparators** ([Linardon et al. 2023](https://centerhealthyminds.org/assets/files-publications/Linardon-et-al-2023-The-efficacy-of-mindfulness-apps-on-symptoms-of-depression-and-anxiety-An-updated-meta-analysis-of-randomized-controlled-trials.pdf)). Attention bias modification is a warning: early medium effects fell to small or non-significant ones in larger analyses ([Cristea et al. 2015](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/efficacy-of-cognitive-bias-modification-interventions-in-anxietyand-depression-metaanalysis/F277CBE661CF8960AF9C2014B42300AB)). For panic, the strongest digital story is a device. Freespira's capnometry-guided breathing has had FDA clearance for adults since 2013 and gained adolescent clearance in 2025, but its high remission figures come from uncontrolled, developer-associated studies ([PMC5344940](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5344940/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250513424670/en/Freespira-Receives-FDA-Clearance-for-Adolescents-Ages-1317-Clinically-Validated-At-Home-Treatment-Reduces-Panic-Disorder-Symptom-Severity-by-54-and-PTSD-Symptom-Severity-by-44)).

The bar for standing out is low, but the bar for credibility is high. Only about **3.4% of 293 anxiety/depression apps** had published efficacy evidence, even though 55% claimed an evidence-based framework ([Marshall et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6872533/)). One well-run, preregistered RCT would place a newcomer in roughly the top 5% of the category. Payers and regulators, however, increasingly expect sham or active controls. Rejoyn's pivotal trial enrolled 373 participants against a sham ([Innolitics K231209](https://510k.innolitics.com/device/K231209)). DaylightRx rested on a 256-person waitlist-controlled RCT published in 2020, followed by clearance about **four years later** ([Psychiatric Times](https://psychiatrictimes.com/view/first-digital-treatment-for-generalized-anxiety-disorder-cleared-by-the-fda)). The notes contain no sourced dollar figures for pivotal digital mental health RCTs. A 200–400 participant trial is plausibly a multi-million-dollar, multi-year commitment, but that is an estimate, not a benchmark.

Generative AI is the newest and least-proven modality. Therabot, the first RCT of a fully generative therapy chatbot (N = 210, NEJM AI, March 2025), cut GAD symptoms by an average **31%** against waitlist over 8 weeks. Staff still had to step in **15 times for safety concerns and 13 times to correct inappropriate responses**, and the lead author said no generative agent is ready to operate autonomously ([Dartmouth](https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits); [JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e78238/PDF)). Pooled generative-chatbot effects are small to moderate (SMD ≈ 0.30–0.36) and transdiagnostic, from a dozen or so short trials ([JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025//e78238/)). A July 2026 scoping review found that engagement declined over time and that safety evaluation was not standardized ([LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/review-finds-mental-health-chatbot-safety-evidence-lags-6f451028)). LLM therapy today is roughly where app CBT stood a decade ago.

## Claims, not code, decide the regulatory bill

In the US the regulatory regime follows the marketing copy. An app that claims only general wellness ("manage everyday stress") stays outside FDA oversight. FDA's revised General Wellness guidance of January 2026 kept the two-factor test of low risk and wellness-only intended use ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products)). Once the app claims to treat GAD or panic disorder, it becomes a **Class II prescription device under 21 CFR 882.5801**. That regulation has a clean 510(k) predicate chain (reSET → Rejoyn → Daylight, K233872), and its special controls require clinical validation and labeling that says the product is an adjunct, not a standalone therapy ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/882.5801); [FDA K233872](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm?ID=K233872)). User fees are minor next to the trial: FY2026 510(k) fees are **$26,067 ($6,517 for small businesses)** and De Novo fees are **$173,782** ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-07-30/html/2025-14412.htm)). An LLM-based "therapist" would probably need a De Novo with no precedent. When FDA's Digital Health Advisory Committee met in November 2025, **FDA had authorized no AI-enabled mental health device**, and the notes found no 2026 guidance on generative-AI mental health devices ([Becker's](https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/behavioral-health-government-policies/fda-panel-reviews-ai-tools-for-mental-health-use-9-notes/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/fdas-digital-health-advisory-committee-weighs-guardrails-for-generative-ai-in-mental-health-devices)).

State law now sets limits on conversational AI that federal law does not. Illinois and Nevada bar AI from independently providing therapy. Utah requires chatbot disclosure and restricts data use and advertising. California's SB 243, in effect since January 1, 2026, and New York's companion-chatbot law require AI disclosure, suicide and self-harm detection, crisis referral and protections for minors. California's law adds a **private right of action** ([Acuity](https://acuity.news/regulation/state-ai-mental-health-laws-compliance-2026/)). Trackers disagree about the 2026 additions in Colorado, Tennessee, Vermont, Rhode Island and Maine, and about how far a federal preemption order reaches ([psychology.com](https://psychology.com/ce/guides/ai-therapy-laws-by-state/)). A scripted CBT app probably sits outside the therapy bans. An empathetic free-text agent could fall under the companion laws.

Privacy enforcement directly limits the standard growth playbook. Most direct-to-consumer apps fall outside HIPAA, but the FTC treats sharing mental health data with ad platforms as unfair or deceptive. BetterHelp paid **$7.8M** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/07/ftc-gives-final-approval-order-banning-betterhelp-sharing-sensitive-health-data-advertising)), Cerebral more than **$7M** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/proposed-ftc-order-will-prohibit-telehealth-firm-cerebral-using-or-disclosing-sensitive-data)), and GoodRx **$1.5M** under the Health Breach Notification Rule ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/02/ftc-enforcement-action-bar-goodrx-sharing-consumers-sensitive-health-info-advertising)). The FTC rescinded its 2021 health-app policy statement on **September 9, 2026**, but the amended 2024 rule and its Section 5 powers remain ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-rescinds-2021-policy-statement-on-health-app-data-breaches)). Washington's My Health My Data Act has a private right of action and has already produced class actions over SDK data leakage ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/first-class-action-filed-under-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act-draws-parallels)). In practice an anxiety app cannot run Meta or TikTok conversion pixels on health events. That undercuts the paid-acquisition engine the consumer model needs.

Tort exposure has become concrete. *Garcia v. Character Technologies* survived a motion to dismiss on product-liability theories and settled on January 7, 2026. As of May 2026, OpenAI was defending at least eight federal suits over psychological harm and wrongful death ([CourtDocket](https://courtdocket.org/garcia-v-character-technologies-ruling-and-settlement/); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonsnyder/2026/05/24/teens-are-dying-by-suicide-openai-lobbies-for-liability-at-100-dead/)). All of these rulings are at the pleading stage, and no merits decision yet sets a duty of care for a mental health app. An anxiety app will still attract users at elevated risk, and a "wellness" label does not stop a negligence claim. Validated risk screening, hard-coded escalation to 988, age gating and a clinical safety hazard log are basic requirements whatever the positioning.

Europe and the UK make the treatment route more formal. A therapeutic anxiety app is almost always **Class IIa under MDR Rule 11**. Vendor estimates put that at about **€60k–200k and 12–24 months** to certify ([Megacert](https://www.megacert.net/blog/mdr-compliance-cost); [Quickbird](https://quickbirdmedical.com/en/medical-device-mdr-risk-class-1-2a/)). An AI component also becomes high-risk under the AI Act. The 2026 Digital Omnibus reportedly moves the Annex I obligations for device-embedded AI to **August 2, 2028**, though the Official Journal dates are not verified ([Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/)). The UK accepted all 44 recommendations of its National Commission on AI in healthcare on October 6, 2026, and is moving AI devices to continuous lifecycle monitoring ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/uk-unveils-new-policy-constantly-monitor-ai-based-medical-devices)). For a non-generative CBT product, these regimes are predictable costs, not existential threats.

## Distribution and retention break the consumer model

Retention is where unguided apps fail. In the most-cited panel study of 93 unguided mental health apps, median **30-day retention was 3.3%**, and breathing-exercise apps had a median of zero ([Baumel et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567)). The notes found no newer independent benchmark, and a 2025 trial still describes real-world retention as below 5% after 30 days ([PMC12089817](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12089817/)). Trials look much better: about **92% uptake, 62% adherence and 19–28% attrition** across 79 RCTs. Attrition was lower with reminders or human support, and it was *higher* in gamified apps ([AJMC on Liu, Torous et al. 2025](https://www.ajmc.com/view/addressing-uptake-adherence-and-attrition-in-mental-health-apps)). Human feedback has cut dropout from about 34% to 12% in depression-app trials ([PubMed 31969272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31969272/)). Engagement therefore depends on the delivery wrapper more than on features. Clinician-embedded deployment is the one lever with consistent evidence behind it.

The economics of a paid subscriber look fine, but the funnel does not. Health & Fitness converts well: median trial-to-paid is **37.7%**, and annual plans bring in 60.6% of revenue ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Acquisition is the binding constraint. US Meta install costs for wellness averaged **$10.10**, with a median month of $6.18 ([Superads](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-app-install/wellness-holistic-health/united-states)). Combine those installs with a low single-digit to ~10% install-to-trial rate and that conversion rate, and my illustrative estimate of the cost per paying subscriber is roughly **$150–400**. That is against a category anchor of about $70 a year ([Sacra](https://www.sacra.com/c/calm)). The market's behavior confirms the arithmetic. Talkspace deliberately shrank its consumer business, with revenue down **29.5% to $17.5M** in FY2025, while payor revenue grew **37.9% to $171.5M** ([Talkspace 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1803901/000119312526105146/talk-20251231.htm)).

The institutional channels are real but thin. Employers buy mental health benefits at about **$3–9 per employee per month**, yet average employer spend fell **~7% in 2025** even as utilization rose ([JD Supra](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/what-to-consider-when-choosing-a-mental-2436167); [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/benefits/mental-health-utilization-is-up--but-employer-spending-fell-7-in-2025-587992.aspx)). Buyers are consolidating around a few platforms, Lyra, Spring Health (now combined with Alma) and Sword–Headspace, which leaves little room for a single-condition point solution selling directly ([ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/mental-health-tech-in-2026-where-the-5b-market-is-consolidating)). In US reimbursement, Medicare's digital mental health treatment codes (G0552–G0554) pay only for devices cleared under 882.5801/882.5803, and CMS itself reports claims volume has "remained low" ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/05/2025-19787/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-cy-2026-payment-policies-under-the-physician-fee-schedule-and-other)). Pear is the cautionary tale. It was cleared but reimbursed on only ~40% of filled prescriptions, posted **~$13M revenue against ~$140M expenses**, and sold its assets for about $6M after Chapter 11 ([Pharmaceutical Technology](https://pharmaceutical-technology.com/analyst-comment/pear-therapeutics-a-lesson-for-future-dtx-developers)). Big Health shows what the surviving version looks like: an Evernorth digital formulary listing, health-system adoption, and a further **$23.7M** raised in February 2026 to keep commercializing ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260212541697/en/Big-Health-Secures-Funding-to-Accelerate-Adoption-of-Digital-Mental-Health-Treatments); [Big Health](https://www.bighealth.com/news/fda-cleared-digital-treatments-are-shaping-the-future-of-mental-healthcare)).

Germany's DiGA is the most concrete reimbursement route, but it is selective and its prices have been compressed. Average manufacturer launch prices of **€544** fall to negotiated prices averaging **€227**. Only **22% of provisional listings** have converted to permanent ([GKV-SV DiGA-Bericht 2025](https://www.gkv-spitzenverband.de/media/dokumente/krankenversicherung_1/telematik/digitales/2025_DiGA_Bericht_GKV_SV.pdf)). Since 2026, at least 20% of the price has to be outcome-linked ([Gawel](https://gawel.ch/insights/50-diga-outcome-based-pricing-digital-therapeutics/)). Psychiatric indications account for about 27–28% of DiGA use, out of roughly 690,000 prescriptions in 2025 ([GKV-SV](https://www.gkv-spitzenverband.de/media/dokumente/krankenversicherung_1/telematik/digitales/2025_DiGA_Bericht_GKV_SV.pdf); [Peix](https://en.peix.de/forward-pharma/diga-im-kreuzfeuer)). A successful anxiety DiGA should therefore plan for thousands to low tens of thousands of prescriptions a year. At 10,000 prescriptions, that is about €2.3M in revenue (illustrative). In England, entry runs through a NICE early value assessment, DTAC and NHS Talking Therapies commissioning, where SilverCloud is already established ([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/mental-health/adults/nhs-talking-therapies/digital/assessment-criteria/)).

## Risk scorecard: the business model, not the science, is the binding constraint

| Dimension | Rating | Main driver | What lowers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market / competition | **High** (DTC) / **Moderate** (regulated subtype niche) | Download-level saturation, low-price meditation incumbents, free ChatGPT; GAD already covered by DaylightRx, SilverCloud and DiGAs | Target a non-GAD indication; sell into platforms instead of against them |
| Clinical evidence | **Moderate** | Small effects (g ≈ 0.2–0.3) that shrink against active controls; RCT-to-clearance takes years | CBT + exposure + monitoring for social anxiety; academic trial partner; sham/active control from the start |
| Regulatory / legal | **Moderate** (scripted CBT) / **High** (generative AI) | Claim-driven FDA/MDR classification, state AI laws, FTC/MHMDA privacy, product-liability suits | Follow the 882.5801 predicate path; no LLM free text in crisis states; no ad pixels; clinical safety governance |
| Business model / distribution | **Very high** | CAC of ~$150–400 vs ~$70/yr pricing; falling employer spend; thin Medicare codes; DiGA price compression | B2B2C licensing; formulary/payer contracts; DiGA/NHS with realistic volume plans |
| User retention | **High** (unguided) / **Moderate** (clinician-embedded) | ~3% day-30 retention for unguided apps; gamification raises attrition | Human support, reminders, measurement-based care, delivery inside a care pathway |

Two caveats temper the scorecard. Evidence risk is low for the *modality* but high for the *timeline*. CBT works, but proving that a specific app works against an active control before the technology goes stale is slow and expensive ([Applied Clinical Trials](https://appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/digital-therapeutics-and-decentralized-trials-a-match-made-in-clinical)). And the risks compound one another. Privacy rules weaken paid acquisition. Weak acquisition forces a move to institutions. Institutions demand RCT-grade evidence. Evidence takes years of runway that today's investors, focused on AI, are reluctant to fund for a non-AI product.

## White space sits in subtypes and inside other people's platforms

The clearest clinical gap is **social anxiety disorder**. It has the strongest app-level effect in the 2024 meta-analysis (g = 0.52), and the notes found no US FDA-cleared product for it. The cleared and recommended incumbents are GAD-centric: DaylightRx's indication, SilverCloud's GAD module and Selfapy's GAD course ([Big Health](https://www.bighealth.com/news/us-fda-grants-clearance-for-daylightrx); [DiGA-Verzeichnis](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/digas/psyche/angststoerung)). The gap is narrower in England, where iCT-SAD sits within NICE's anxiety assessment, and the broad German anxiety DiGAs may already cover social anxiety under general labels. Because the 882.5801 classification already exists, a social-anxiety indication could plausibly go through 510(k) rather than De Novo. **Panic disorder** looks open but is a riskier gap. Pooled app effects are null, Mindable appears to have left the DiGA directory, and Freespira's device holds the evidence-backed position. The gap may exist because the problem is hard, not because nobody has tried. **Health anxiety** and specific populations are open by inference only. The notes found no direct demand data. Indications skew to working-age adults (DaylightRx 22+, Invirto 18–65), and the UK has a formal under-18 route through NICE HTE3 that the US lacks ([NICE HTE3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/hte3/chapter/1-Recommendations)).

The commercial white space is structural rather than clinical. Buyers such as Sword, UHS and the employer platforms are assembling "front door" mental-health platforms with clinician networks ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/09/16/sword-acquires-headspace-300m-all-cash-ai-care-mental-health-platform/)). They need protocolized, condition-specific modules that report validated outcome measures (GAD-7, SPIN, PDSS) and hand off cleanly to clinicians. There is also a positioning gap between unregulated LLMs and retreating regulated chatbots: a **protocol-bound, clinically governed CBT product that uses AI for personalization and triage, not autonomous therapy**. That space is contested. Wysa holds much of it in the NHS and with payers, and Slingshot is well funded. A newcomer will win there on indication focus and evidence, not on the AI itself.

| 1 | **B2B2C module licensed into platforms, EAPs and health plans** (social-anxiety or panic protocol with measurement-based care) | Most viable | Uses buyers' existing distribution and clinicians; matches the consolidation trend; low regulatory burden if claims stay coping-skills/adjunct. Per-user economics are low (health plans ~$0.85–2.50 PMPM, low-confidence ([PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/ik0105))) |

| 3 | **US prescription DTx via 510(k) under 882.5801**, sold via PBM formularies and health systems first, with Medicare DMHT as a supplement | Viable only with deep, patient capital | Predicate path is clean and fees are small, but Pear shows clearance without coverage is fatal and Big Health shows it takes repeated raises |
| 4 | **Freemium consumer app as an evidence and lead-generation funnel** feeding a clinical or B2B offer | Marginal | Useful for building real-world evidence and brand, but cannot carry the company on its own; ad-tech restrictions limit growth |
| 5 | **Direct sales of a single-condition point solution to employers** | Weak | Falling employer spend, vendor consolidation, outcomes demands and 4–18 month cycles favour incumbents |
| 6 | **DTC subscription anxiety app** | Least viable | ~3% day-30 retention, CAC likely above first-year revenue, Calm and ChatGPT as anchors; Talkspace and Calm are pulling back from this channel |
| 7 | **Autonomous generative-AI "anxiety therapist"**, DTC | Least viable | Banned in some states, companion-law duties and a private right of action in California, live product-liability theories, no FDA precedent; Woebot exited this space |

## Conclusion

The finding that matters is that **the moat in anxiety apps has moved from content to institutional standing**. In 2020 a good app with good content could build a brand. In 2026 content is a commodity, and conversation is free at the scale of 800 million weekly users. What remains scarce is a validated, indication-specific protocol that a payer, a national health system or a platform can plug in, measure and defend to a regulator. An entrant should therefore pick an indication before picking features, design the first trial around an active or sham control, keep generative AI out of crisis handling and out of the "therapist" role, and choose a distribution partner before choosing a launch market.

The biggest uncertainty is timing on the AI side. If FDA issues usable guidance for generative mental health devices, or if a well-governed LLM product wins clearance, the regulated tier could reprice quickly in favor of AI-native players. Litigation against general-purpose chatbots, on the other hand, could push consumers and payers toward clinically governed alternatives. Neither outcome makes a standalone consumer anxiety app viable. Both make an evidence-backed, subtype-specific module more valuable to whoever owns distribution. On current evidence the most likely good outcome for a new entrant is a licensing deal or an acquisition, not a market-leading consumer brand.
